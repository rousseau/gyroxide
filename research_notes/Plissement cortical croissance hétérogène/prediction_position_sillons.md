# Predictability of sulcal location from earlier cortical geometry or prepatterns (developing human brain)

Context: in our fitted mechanical model, a growth law driven by the local sulcal depth of the dHCP fetal atlas one week earlier (SG 26) keeps most of the gain when predicting SG 27 folds. The question is whether the literature supports the idea that future fold positions are already present in earlier geometry, and how stable sulcal positions are across gestational weeks.

Verification note: unless marked otherwise, every reference below was checked against PubMed metadata (title, journal, DOI) or the Crossref API (DOI, volume, pages). Journal-issue years are given; the online-first year sometimes differs (for example Lohmann: online 2007, issue 2008).

---

## 1. Sulcal pits as early, positionally stable landmarks (adult, infant, fetal)

### Takeaway
Sulcal pits (local depth maxima within sulcal basins) sit in the same places across individuals. Their positions stay stable from term birth to 2 years (Meng 2014), and in fetuses from about 22 to 32 GW they are statistically indistinguishable across gestational-age groups and resemble adult positions (Yun 2020). They are therefore the best-documented evidence that the deepest early fold points mark where sulci will be, and stay, during later expansion. Their heritability is only moderate (h² about 0.2–0.5, Le Guen 2018), so the "strict genetic control" interpretation is partly an assumption.

### Cited Findings
- **Lohmann et al., Cereb Cortex 2008;18:1415–1420** ("Deep sulcal landmarks provide an organizing framework for human cortical folding"; 96 adults). Along each sulcus there is generally one well-defined zone of maximal depth, the "sulcal pit"; long sulci have 2–3. The pits form a "strikingly regular" arrangement of alternating chains of deeper and shallower pits. The authors start from the premise that the deepest parts of primary sulci "are the first to develop embryologically and change least as the cortex expands". They hypothesise that pits are encoded in Rakic's protomap; this is not tested. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhm174](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhm174)
- **Im et al., Cereb Cortex 2010;20:602–611** (148 adults). Pit spatial distribution is "relatively invariant between individuals". Left and right pits are spatially covariant in the regions of the earliest-developing sulci. The strongest asymmetry in pit frequency and spatial variance is in the superior temporal sulcus (STS). — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhp127](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhp127)
- **Im et al., PLoS One 2013**. Pit extraction is reliable: presence similarity is above 0.90 and spatial difference below 1.7 mm across sessions and scanners; across surface tools it is about 0.85 and about 2.0 mm. — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0053678](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0053678)
- **Auzias et al., NeuroImage 2015**. Optimises the parameters of the Im 2010 extraction (the depth-potential function/watershed pipeline used in later fetal work) to make pit counts per region reproducible. Benchmark data are in Data Brief ([DOI 10.1016/j.dib.2015.10.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2015.10.004)). — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.02.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.02.008)
- **Meng et al., NeuroImage 2014** (73 infants, each scanned longitudinally at birth, 1 year and 2 years). The spatially consistent pit distribution in major sulci "already existed at term birth" and "keeps relatively stable in the first 2 years of life, despite that the cerebral cortex expands dramatically and the sulcal depth increases considerably". Pit depth grows regionally heterogeneously: faster in prefrontal and temporal association cortex than in sensorimotor cortex. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.06.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.06.004)
- **Meng et al., Hum Brain Mapp 2018** (677 neonates). Major neonatal sulcal patterns are mined from pit distributions, on the premise that deep pits are "genetically related, relatively consistent across individuals, and also stable during development". — [DOI 10.1002/hbm.24199](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24199)
- **Im & Grant, NeuroImage 2019;185:881–890** (review, "Sulcal pits and patterns in developing human brains"). Early sulcal folds and pits (putative first folds) are hypothesised to be under stronger genetic control and more tied to functional areas than later sulci. Graph-based sulcal-pattern analysis detects subtle fetal abnormalities, for example in polymicrogyria and agenesis of the corpus callosum. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.057](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.057)
- **Yun et al., Cereb Cortex 2020;30(7):4257** (48 typically developing fetuses, 22.0–32.0 GW, cross-sectional; three groups: 22.0–24.7, 25.1–28.0 and 28.6–32.0 GW).
  - Pit positions within each cluster showed "no significant difference ... among the subgroups" (Kruskal–Wallis on 1-D PCA coordinates).
  - Per-cluster positional SD correlated across fetal groups: early vs mid r=0.59, mid vs late r=0.70, early vs late r=0.50. Fetal SD also correlated with adult SD (mid r=0.55, late r=0.31).
  - The abstract states that fetal pit position and spatial variance "are similar to those in the adult brain" and are "temporally uniform against dynamic brain growth".
  - Emergence is staggered. Pit frequency rises significantly between the 22–25 GW and 25–28 GW groups for the central, precentral b, postcentral b, calcarine b, orbital, left STS b, left intraparietal a and right parieto-occipital clusters. It rises between the 25–28 GW and 28–32 GW groups for the middle frontal (MFS), intraparietal b, right inferior temporal and right lateral occipital clusters.
  - Sylvian, cingulate, STS a, calcarine a and collateral pits are already present before 22 GW.
  - Caveats: the fetal–adult comparison is mostly visual, and no mm distances are reported. (Numbers from the full text via the OUP page.) — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhaa053](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhaa053); [full text](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/30/7/4257/5815453)
- **Yun et al., NeuroImage 2022** (fetal sulcal emergence timing, logistic fits). Emergence proceeds from central to temporo-parieto-occipital and then frontal regions. Inter-individual timing variability is mostly 1–2 weeks: under 1 week for the left central and postcentral sulci, over 2 weeks for the occipitotemporal and left STS. Variability increases with emergence time. The right STS emerges before the left. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119629](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119629)
- **Le Guen et al., Cereb Cortex 2018** (Human Connectome Project pedigree). Pit-depth heritability ranges from 0.2 to 0.5, regionally and with an asymmetry in the STS. The authors conclude that "cortical shape is determined primarily by nongenetic factors", while genetic cues still play a role in pit formation. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhx098](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhx098)
- **Le Guen et al., NeuroImage 2018**. A "highly reproducible grid of sulcal pits across individuals" is used to extract plis de passage. Only a few interruptions are genetically constrained: collateral, postcentral, STS and calcarine. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.046](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.046)
- **Régis et al., Neurol Med Chir 2005;45:1–17** ("Sulcal root" generic model). This is the conceptual precursor: primary folds are organised around stable "sulcal roots", the elementary first-forming dimples, and these later merge into variable sulci. — [DOI 10.2176/nmc.45.1](https://doi.org/10.2176/nmc.45.1) (verified via Crossref; content summarised from Lefèvre & Mangin 2010, below, which cites it)
- **Kaltenmark et al., Med Image Anal 2020**. Group-level parcellation into sulcal basins around pits, giving pit correspondence across subjects. — [DOI 10.1016/j.media.2020.101749](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2020.101749)

### Inferences
- If pit positions are invariant across 22–32 GW and their positional spread barely changes, then the position of a sulcus that is already a shallow dimple at SG 26 is a good predictor of its SG 27 position. This is consistent with our depth(SG 26) growth law keeping most of the benefit.
- The predictive power should be uneven across regions. Sulci whose pits mostly emerge between about 25–28 and 28–32 GW (MFS, intraparietal b, inferior temporal, lateral occipital, part of the precentral) are partly absent or very shallow at SG 26. The SG 26 depth map cannot encode them, unless subtle sub-millimetre curvature already does. Residual errors of the SG 26 law should concentrate there.
- Inter-individual emergence-timing variability is 1–2 weeks (Yun 2022). An atlas, which is an average, at SG 26 therefore already blurs sulci that individuals form between SG 25 and SG 28. In an atlas-to-atlas setting (SG 26 to SG 27) this blurring acts like a smooth spatial prior, which may help rather than hurt.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal fetal study gives within-subject pit displacement in mm per week. Yun 2020 is cross-sectional. Solhtalab 2025 (section 3) is longitudinal but reports a similarity score, not distances.
- "Pits are genetically encoded in a protomap" is an untested hypothesis in Lohmann 2008, Im 2010 and Yun 2020. Measured heritability is moderate (0.2–0.5).

## 2. Fetal and preterm folding trajectories, spectral analysis and reaction-diffusion (Lefèvre, Germanaud, Dubois, Habas, Clouchoux, Wright, Garcia)

### Takeaway
Imaging studies show a stereotyped, regionally staged sequence: primary, then secondary, then tertiary waves; rapid folding between about 25 and 30 GW; most central-sulcus shape features already set at 30 wPMA. Local surface expansion maps anticipate where new folds appear. Phenomenological models (reaction-diffusion; mean-curvature-flow "smoothing back") treat early geometry or early "roots" as the initial condition that organises later folds. This supports predicting from earlier geometry, but no imaging study directly regresses future fold position on earlier depth or curvature fields.

### Cited Findings
- **Lefèvre & Mangin, PLoS Comput Biol 2010** (reaction-diffusion model). Turing morphogens drive differential growth of sulcal and gyral areas on a finite-element surface. Several noisy realisations give reproducible folds, but the same fold can vary topologically. The authors link this to the sulcal-root theory: "These sulcal roots would correspond to initial conditions in our model." Parameter changes produce polymicrogyria- and lissencephaly-like patterns. — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000749](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000749)
- **Lefèvre et al., MICCAI 2013** ("Surface smoothing: a way back in early brain morphogenesis"). Cortical smoothing by mean-curvature flow is used as a proxy for running development backwards. A one-parameter model fits the volume–area developmental trajectory of 40 preterm surfaces, and central-sulcus trajectories match along developmental and simulation time. This implies that earlier stages are approximately a smoothed (low-pass) version of later geometry. — PubMed lists only the volume DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-40810-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-40810-6); chapter DOI not verified
- **Lefèvre et al., Cereb Cortex 2016;26:3023–3035** ("Are developmental trajectories of cortical folding comparable between cross-sectional datasets of fetuses and preterm newborns?"; 21–36 GA weeks). Apart from cortical volume and the rate of sulcal appearance, global folding metrics follow different trajectories in fetuses and preterm newborns, so the shift from in utero to ex utero visibly affects morphology. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhv123](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhv123)
  - Relevance: dHCP fetal (in utero) and neonatal or preterm data should not be mixed naively when fitting week-to-week growth laws.
- **Germanaud et al., NeuroImage 2012** (SPANGY: spectral analysis of gyrification). Mean curvature is decomposed on Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions into 7 bands (B0–B6). B4, B5 and B6 carry 93% of the power and correspond to primary, secondary and tertiary folds respectively. In larger brains only the third-order (B6) elements add new pattern elements; the B4 contribution is constant across brain sizes. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.07.053](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.07.053)
- **Germanaud et al., NeuroImage 2014** (microcephalies). Gyral simplification is mostly explained by allometric scaling. An additional disease effect appears in ASPM- and PQBP1-linked microcephaly. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.07.057](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.07.057)
- **Dubois et al., NeuroImage 2018 (PubMed year)** (SPANGY applied to 58 preterm and term infants, 27–62 wPMA). Developmental trajectories reveal "three successive waves that might correspond to primary, secondary and tertiary folding", with deviations in preterm infants at term-equivalent age. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.03.005)
- **Dubois et al., Cereb Cortex 2008** (35 preterm, 26–36 weeks). Maps sulcal emergence in vivo, with inter-individual variability in timing and a larger right than left STS. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhm180](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhm180)
- **Dubois et al., Brain 2008** (45 preterm: singletons, twins, IUGR). Twins show delayed but harmonious maturation. Folding in IUGR is discordant from the normal trajectory. Folding measures at birth predict term-equivalent outcome. — [DOI 10.1093/brain/awn137](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awn137)
- **de Vareilles et al., NeuroImage 2021** (71 extremely preterm infants scanned at about 30 wPMA and about 40 wPMA). Of ten central-sulcus shape features, 8 are already "encoded as early as 30w PMA". The adult-like asymmetry is visible at term. This is direct longitudinal evidence that fold shape, not just position, is set about 10 weeks earlier. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118837](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118837)
- **de Vareilles et al., Dev Cogn Neurosci 2023** (review of folding hypotheses and early neuroimaging). — [DOI 10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101249](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101249)
- **Habas et al., Cereb Cortex 2012;22:13–25** (in utero MRI, 20–28 GW). Gives precise temporal staging of the folding changes in each region and the emergence of interhemispheric asymmetries during the window when primary sulci form. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhr053](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhr053)
- **Rajagopalan et al., J Neurosci 2011** (40 fetuses, 20–28 GW; tensor-based morphometry). Increased local relative growth rates in the forming precentral and postcentral gyri, right superior temporal gyrus and opercula. Gyrification-related growth is more pronounced after 24 GW. — [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5458-10.2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5458-10.2011)
- **Clouchoux et al., Brain Struct Funct 2012;217:127–139** (fetuses at 25–35 GW, in vivo). Explicit sulcal-pattern delineation on the surface. Gyrification in the third trimester is "exuberant", and sulcal development appears non-linear. — [DOI 10.1007/s00429-011-0325-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-011-0325-x)
- **Clouchoux et al., Cereb Cortex 2012 (PubMed year)** (delayed cortical development in fetuses with congenital heart disease). — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhs281](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhs281) (title and DOI verified; content not read)
- **Wright et al., NeuroImage 2014;91:21–32** (80 fetuses, 21.7–38.9 GW). Curvature-based folding measures correlate with GA at R²=0.99. Folding "increases rapidly between 25 and 30 weeks" and then slows. Regional Gompertz fits show parietal and posterior temporal regions folding fastest and cingulate, frontal and medial temporal regions slowest. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.01.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.01.034)
- **Wright et al., NeuroImage 2015** (spatio-temporal fetal surface atlas by spectral matching). Weekly templates; sulcal alignment with average Fréchet distance about 0.4 mm at 30 weeks. — [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.05.087](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.05.087)
- **Garcia et al., PNAS 2018;115:3156–3161** (30 preterm infants scanned 2–4 times at 28–38 wPMA; anatomically constrained MSM, aMSM). Smooth maps of local cortical expansion show regional differences "consistent with the emergence of new folds". Growth patterns shift over development, and uninjured subjects follow "a highly consistent trajectory". — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1715451115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1715451115)
- **da Silva et al., bioRxiv 2025** (dHCP fetal and preterm, 22–44 wPMA; aMSM). Distinct regional and temporal expansion patterns in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters. Preterm ex-utero growth follows a modified trajectory. Preprint, not peer-reviewed. — [DOI 10.1101/2025.05.19.653958](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.19.653958)
- **Mihailov et al., Commun Biol 2025** (819 sessions, 21–45 wPMA). A burst of gyrification immediately after birth amounts to about half of the whole fetal gyrification increase. — [DOI 10.1038/s42003-025-08155-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08155-z)

### Inferences
- The SPANGY picture supports the SG 26 depth law. The low-frequency (B4, primary) component is the earliest and the most size-invariant. Future primary sulci at a coarse scale should therefore already be readable in the low-pass geometry one week earlier, while secondary and tertiary (B5, B6) elements are what an earlier-depth law will miss.
- The Lefèvre 2013 idea that curvature flow runs development backwards is the mirror image of our approach. If SG 26 is roughly a smoothed SG 27, the depth or curvature of SG 26 is a smoothed indicator of where SG 27 sulci lie. That makes the depth(SG 26) law close to a "smoothed target" prior. This is worth stating explicitly as a possible circularity: the law partly encodes the answer.
- Garcia 2018 and Rajagopalan 2011 indicate that local expansion is itself spatially prepatterned and anticipates folds. A depth-based law may therefore be a proxy for an underlying expansion field.

### Gaps
- I found no study that quantitatively predicts week-(n+1) sulcal positions from week-n depth or curvature maps in fetal atlases, for example with an ROC of depth(n) against sulcal labels(n+1). This appears to be an open niche.
- Lefèvre et al. 2016 warns about in-utero versus ex-utero differences. I did not find a dHCP-fetal-atlas-specific analysis of week-to-week sulcal displacement.

## 3. Mechanical models predicting fold location from initial geometry or curvature

### Takeaway
Mechanical models agree that differential tangential growth triggers folding. Where folds go is strongly set by initial geometry: existing undulations or primary folds, overall brain shape and thickness. Spatially heterogeneous growth or axonal density then modulate this. Several models start from a real fetal MRI geometry: 22 GW for Tallinen 2016 and Hou 2026, 26–31 GW for Solhtalab 2025. They reproduce major sulci or later pit patterns, and Solhtalab 2025 is a direct within-subject longitudinal test.

### Cited Findings
- **Tallinen et al., PNAS 2014**. Gyrification follows from tangential cortical expansion constrained by white matter. Patterns depend on relative expansion and relative thickness. — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1406015111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1406015111)
- **Tallinen, Chung, Rousseau, Girard, Lefèvre & Mahadevan, Nature Physics 2016;12:588–593** ("On the growth and form of cortical convolutions").
  - Numerical simulations and a 3D-printed swelling-gel replica start from MRI of a smooth fetal brain.
  - The authors conclude that the size, shape, placement and orientation of folds arise from an elementary mechanical instability "modulated by early fetal brain geometry".
  - Sources: DOI and author list verified via Crossref and Semantic Scholar. The geometry-modulation statement is confirmed by a web-search summary; the abstract was not directly accessible (paywall/HAL access denied).
  - The 22 GW initial geometry and the qualitative match of primary sulci come from prior knowledge and are **not re-verified here**.
  - — [DOI 10.1038/nphys3632](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys3632); [HAL hal-01342578](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01342578)
- **Razavi et al., Sci Rep 2015**. The cortex-to-core growth ratio, initial thickness and material properties strongly shape the patterns. The paper discusses why folding is consistent. — [DOI 10.1038/srep14477](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep14477)
- **Zhang et al., Sci Rep 2016**. "Global differential growth can only produce a random gyrification pattern, but it cannot guarantee gyrus formation at certain locations"; regions growing faster consistently produce gyri. This is the counterpoint: under uniform growth, location is set by geometry or noise. — [DOI 10.1038/srep37272](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep37272)
- **Chavoshnejad et al., Sci Rep 2023** ("Mechanical hierarchy in the formation and modulation of cortical folding patterns"; bilayer spheres).
  - Differential tangential growth "is the inducer of cortical folding". High-amplitude initial undulations and axonal fibres "regulate the folding patterns and determine the location of gyri and sulci".
  - There is a "strong correlation between the location of positive (outward) and negative (inward) initial undulations and the locations of gyri and sulci after folding, respectively".
  - 3-hinge gyri correlate with positive undulations and dense axons. — [DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-40086-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-40086-9) (PMC10425471)
- **Chavoshnejad et al., Cereb Cortex 2023**. A GAN surrogate trained on finite-element simulations predicts folded morphology from a given initial curvature configuration, including 3-hinges. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhad208](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhad208)
- **Solhtalab et al., Hum Brain Mapp 2025** ("Mechanics of the spatiotemporal evolution of sulcal pits in the folding brain"; PMC12381649).
  - Data: 24 fetuses, each with 2 MRIs. Timepoint 1 is about 26–31 GW (table 26.7–31.9); timepoint 2 is about 33–39.6 GW, with text and table slightly inconsistent.
  - Model: a true-scale neo-Hookean model is built from the timepoint-1 MRI, with uniform thickness 1.4 mm and a single homogeneous growth ratio G_ctx/G_sub = 4. There is no spatially varying growth. It is grown until it reaches the timepoint-2 volume.
  - Matching: pits are extracted with DPF and watershed and matched by reweighted random walk; the similarity score (SDSP) is used to identify the correct subject.
  - Early MRI vs later MRI identifies the true match in 11 of 24 cases. FEM-predicted vs later MRI does so in 17 of 24.
  - In 7 failed cases, the number of off-diagonal values exceeding the true match drops from 12.29±7.76 to 5.57±6.27 (p=0.028).
  - The authors conclude that "sulcal pits are stable during brain development" and that "initial surface undulations (primary folds) during early developmental stages serve as critical locations" for later gyri and sulci.
  - Caveats: no mm distances; small selected sample; scan quality and gap length affect results. — [DOI 10.1002/hbm.70332](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.70332); [PMC12381649](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12381649/)
- **Hou et al., Nat Commun 2026** ("Biophysical modeling of anatomically realistic prenatal cortical folding development").
  - Setup: 22 GW dHCP-derived surfaces. Region-specific tangential and radial growth laws are obtained by symbolic regression on 120 dHCP scans (22–40 GW).
  - Outputs: reproduces the central, postcentral, intraparietal, superior temporal, lateral and inferior frontal sulci; vertex-wise shape-index similarity above 0.8 (n=10); primary folds stabilise around 32 GW.
  - Ablations: uniform isotropic growth gives fewer, bulkier folds. Tangential regional growth matches best, and heterogeneous tangential growth is called "indispensable". Changing the growth trajectory shifts timing but leaves the final pattern "largely similar".
  - Discussion: primary folds are "more strongly governed by geometrical factors" while growth is a "dynamic modulator".
  - Caveats: no Dice or landmark-error numbers are reported. — [DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-75582-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75582-9) (PMC13490475; figures from full text via WebFetch, first ~75% read)
- **Balouchzadeh, Bayly & Garcia, Brain Multiphysics 2023**. Stress-dependent subcortical growth gives a better match to the sulcal direction and curvature evolution of 4 preterm infants scanned at 4 time points. Initial shape (sphere or spheroids) sets sulcal orientation. — [DOI 10.1016/j.brain.2023.100065](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brain.2023.100065)
- **Razavi et al., Front Comput Neurosci 2017**. Proposes that regionally heterogeneous radial glia distribution (Trnp1-controlled) and axons scaffold gyral and sulcal positions. — [DOI 10.3389/fncom.2017.00076](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00076)
- **Wang et al., IEEE EMBC 2019**. A longitudinal growth model and the effect of initial cortical thickness on folding, quantified with GI and SPANGY. — [DOI 10.1109/EMBC.2019.8856670](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC.2019.8856670)

### Inferences
- Solhtalab 2025 is the closest published analogue to our result. With homogeneous growth and only the earlier geometry, a mechanical model identifies later pit patterns better than the raw earlier MRI does (17 of 24 vs 11 of 24). Earlier geometry plus mechanics therefore carries the location information.
- Our setup goes further: a one-week step on an atlas, with a growth law tied to SG 26 depth. It tests whether earlier geometry is useful as a growth field, not just as an initial condition. This separates two mechanisms that Hou 2026 and Chavoshnejad 2023 discuss: geometric seeding (initial undulations) and growth modulation (heterogeneous expansion).
- A useful ablation, if not already done: the same model with homogeneous growth from the SG 26 initial geometry. The difference between that and the depth(SG 26) law isolates how much information enters through the growth field rather than through the initial shape. Hou 2026 and Zhang 2016 suggest both contribute.

### Gaps
- No mechanical study reports quantitative positional error (mm) of predicted vs observed sulci over a 1-week fetal interval.
- I found no model that uses earlier sulcal depth as the spatial modulator of growth (our approach). Closest are data-driven regional growth (Hou 2026), local expansion maps (Garcia 2018) and initial undulations (Chavoshnejad 2023).

## 4. Molecular and cellular protomaps and genetic prepatterns of fold location

### Takeaway
The protomap hypothesis (Rakic 1988) was formulated for cytoarchitectonic areas. Its extension to folds is supported mainly by ferret work: discrete gene-expression domains in germinal layers mark prospective folds and fissures before folding (de Juan Romero 2015). In ferret the transcriptomic folding protomap starts at early embryonic stages, and manipulating one regulator changes the folding pattern (Singh 2024). In humans the link from protomap to sulcal pits is still inferred, not shown.

### Cited Findings
- **Rakic, Science 1988** ("Specification of cerebral cortical areas"). The ventricular proliferative units provide "a proto-map of prospective cytoarchitectonic areas", translated to the cortex via radial glial guides. — [DOI 10.1126/science.3291116](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.3291116)
- **Rakic, Trends Neurosci 2009** ("Decision by division: making cortical maps"). Updates the radial-unit and protomap hypotheses. — [DOI 10.1016/j.tins.2009.01.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2009.01.007)
- **de Juan Romero et al., EMBO J 2015;34:1859–1874** ("Discrete domains of gene expression in germinal layers distinguish the development of gyrencephaly").
  - Transcriptomics of individual germinal layers in ferret, comparing regions prospective of fold vs fissure: "thousands of genes are differentially expressed between regions, including ~80% of genes mutated in human cortical malformations".
  - Discrete expression domains occur in ferret and human but not in the lissencephalic mouse. They "emerge late during development and map the eventual location of folds or fissures". — [DOI 10.15252/embj.201591176](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201591176) (PMC4547892; abstract via Europe PMC)
- **Singh, Del-Valle-Anton, de Juan Romero, ..., Borrell, Sci Adv 2024** ("Gene regulatory landscape of cerebral cortex folding").
  - Primary folding "occurs in highly stereotyped patterns that are predefined in the cortical germinal zones by a transcriptomic protomap", which "begins to emerge at early embryonic stages" in ferret.
  - The H3K27ac epigenetic landscape is involved, and manipulating a key transcription factor (name stripped in the PubMed abstract) in progenitors changed proliferation and the folding pattern. — [DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adn1640](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn1640)
- **Donoghue & Rakic, Cereb Cortex 1999**. Molecular gradients and compartments in the embryonic primate cortex. — [DOI 10.1093/cercor/9.6.586](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/9.6.586) (title verified; content not read)

### Inferences
- If prospective sulcal and gyral locations are prepatterned in progenitor zones weeks before folding, the earliest geometric signal (shallow dimples, local curvature, differential local expansion) is a downstream readout of that prepattern. An "earlier geometry" growth law is then a practical proxy for the molecular map. This is consistent with our SG 26 result, but it does not prove geometry is causal.
- Moderate pit heritability (Le Guen 2018) and mechanical results that only geometry plus noise set location under uniform growth (Zhang 2016) suggest the human system combines a coarse genetic prepattern for primary sulci with geometry- and mechanics-driven placement of later folds.

### Gaps
- There is no direct human evidence (fetal spatial transcriptomics aligned with in-utero MRI sulcal maps) linking germinal-zone expression domains to specific sulcal pits.
- Timing: de Juan Romero 2015 says the domains emerge "late during development", while Singh 2024 says the protomap begins "at early embryonic stages". These may refer to different gene sets or stages; I did not check the full texts to reconcile them.
- **Unverified or not found:** a paper by "Kriegstein" specifically on fold-location prepatterns. Kriegstein's work concerns outer radial glia and progenitor diversity, and I did not locate a fold-position-specific study. A "Meng et al. 2014 infant sulcal pits" paper exists and is verified above. "Yun et al." fetal pits is verified as Yun 2020 and 2022.
